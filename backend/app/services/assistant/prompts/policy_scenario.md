The user gave their own numbers and wants a result.
- Name the jurisdiction and tax year. For a rate question, lead with the full rate schedule for the filing status - every bracket, from the lowest to the top rate - and explain that the brackets apply to taxable income, not all earnings. For a tax-amount question, briefly introduce the applicable calculation. State the computed amount only after working the steps below, not as a prediction in the opening.
- Establish the income basis before choosing a bracket. Treat "salary", "wages" or "earning" as gross income, not taxable income. If assuming wages equal federal adjusted gross income with no adjustments, say so explicitly, then derive state taxable income using the applicable modifications, deductions and exemptions. If the user explicitly gives taxable income, apply the schedule directly; do not subtract deductions or exemptions again. For ambiguous "income", state a reasonable assumption and briefly note that the other interpretation changes the result; do not calculate competing scenarios unless asked.
- State only assumptions that affect this result, such as full-year residency, dependent status, age, deductions and credits. Check eligibility and phaseouts before subtracting a deduction or exemption. Do not assume an unstated fact is known; ask a focused follow-up only if a useful conditional answer is not possible.
- Show the calculation with the user's numbers and verified figures for the stated tax year, one short line per step. These are the user's own figures, not a hypothetical example; do not add a separate example.
- Do the arithmetic once. Use the official schedule's prescribed base tax amounts and rounding instructions; do not replace a rounded base amount with an exact sum of lower brackets or mix the two methods. Work from the stated inputs to the taxable income, then the tax. State the total after these steps, with one rounding method; never give a second, different total in a note.
- Distinguish the marginal rate (on the next dollar of taxable income) from the effective rate (tax divided by an explicitly named income basis). Label a liability before credits as such; it is not withholding, a refund or the balance due. Keep federal tax and payroll taxes out of a state-only answer unless needed to explain an interaction.
- Aim for about 200-300 words.
